Overview

Daily work here, then a clear handoff home.

This program is not open yet. We are building a small kennel on our property in Meteghan, and we will offer board-and-train when it is ready.

We are starting small: two indoor kennels, each with its own outdoor run.

Board and Train is not the same product as our owner-led Functional Obedience course. In Functional Obedience, you come to the sessions and practise at home between them. We teach you as much as we teach your dog. In Board and Train, your dog stays with us for a stay that matches that program. We do the daily installing here. Then we teach you how to run it when your dog comes home.

Ten owner-led sessions are not the same as daily training during a stay. We will not claim they produce the same results.

Who it is for

  • Adolescent and adult dogs that need dependable everyday manners.
  • Owners who want those skills installed through daily work while the dog stays with us.
  • Families who are ready to learn the handoff, so the work holds up at home after the stay.
  • Anyone considering this program should start with a free consultation.

What is covered

The same everyday manners, installed through daily work.

The stay is built to match our Functional Obedience program. Your dog works the same skills. The difference is how often that work happens, and who is doing it day to day.

What your dog will learn

  • Foundation: charging the terminal marker, shaping positions with food, bridging to verbal cues and leash pressure
  • Sit, down, stand, place and heel: on one cue, each held until released with your release word. We do not teach a separate stay command. Each position includes the hold. We build duration by waiting a little longer before “yes”, using “good” as a bridge early on and fading it as the hold becomes reliable. “Yes” ends the behaviour and starts the reward event. “Okay” releases with no reward implied.
  • Recall on a long line, with leash pressure backing the cue “come”
  • Loose-leash walking, on and off the property
  • A daily structured walk, with breaks to sniff on your release
  • Doorway and greeting manners: waiting at doors, gates and the vehicle, and calm greetings with four paws on the floor
  • Engagement: paying attention to the handler and coming back after a distraction
  • Leave it, and neutrality around other dogs, people and busy places, kept under threshold
  • Optional extras: crate training, a muzzle introduction (teaching your dog to be happy wearing a muzzle, never forced) and an e-collar introduction. Not scored and not needed to finish the stay. Read why we recommend crate training and muzzle training.

What you will learn

  • How to take the work over when your dog comes home
  • Handler consistency and clear cues: one clear cue, consistent rules
  • Timing, reward placement and leash handling
  • How to keep using leash pressure as guidance, and when a bump or a pop is the right tool
  • Spotting when your dog is struggling
  • How to keep the training going on your own

Daily work

Short sessions, rest, movement and clear structure.

  • Multiple short training sessions through the day, rather than one long weekly lesson
  • Rest and calm downtime on place and in the crate
  • Outdoor run time for toileting and movement
  • Structured walks. We use leash pressure as guidance: a low, steady feel on the collar to show your dog the way. A bump is a soft pulsing tug for direction, not a correction. A leash pop is a correction, and we use it only when the work calls for it.
  • Regular meals on a steady schedule

Food during the stay

Boarded dogs eat Royal Canin unless you provide your dog’s usual food. If your dog is already on a food that agrees with it, send that food with your dog.

How it differs

Owner-led lessons and a stay with us are not the same thing.

Functional Obedience

Our owner-led course. You attend the sessions with your dog and practise at home between them. We teach skills, cues, a daily rhythm, and real-life proofing with you in the picture from the start. It is 10 sessions.

See Functional Obedience

Board and Train

Your dog stays with us. We install the same Functional Obedience skills through daily work. The stay length matches that program. When your dog goes home, handoff lessons teach you how to run the training, so the work does not stay behind at our place.

How it works

Consult, stay, handoff, then you take over.

  1. Consultation. We meet you and your dog, talk through what you want, and decide whether a stay is the right fit. A free walk-in consultation is the place to start, and we can also come to your home anywhere in the Tri-County area (Digby, Clare and Yarmouth).
  2. The stay. Your dog lives with us for a stay that matches our Functional Obedience program. Days are built around short training sessions, rest, outdoor run time and structured walks.
  3. Daily installing. We teach the skills listed above, using the same cues you will use at home: “yes”, “good”, “okay”, sit, down, stand, place, heel, come, crate and the rest of our everyday language.
  4. Handoff lessons. Before and as your dog goes home, we show you how to run the work: one clear cue, consistent rules, and the same leash handling your dog already understands.
  5. Home. The goal is a dog that can do the work with you, not only with us. If something breaks down after the handoff, we use that information to adjust the plan.
