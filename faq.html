Getting started

How do I get started with dog training at Acadia Canine Academy?

Send us a message through the contact form or call (905) 517-5473 and tell us a little about your dog. We usually reply within 1 to 2 business days to set up a consultation. From there we can recommend a course or a plan of hourly sessions that fits your dog and your goals.

What happens at a first consultation?

We meet you and your dog, ask about daily routines and the problems you want solved, and watch how your dog handles the training field. You leave with an honest read on where your dog is today, a clear plan and a few things to practise at home. Consultations are $60 CAD per hour.

What is balanced dog training?

Balanced training uses all four ways dogs learn: adding something good, taking away something unpleasant, adding something the dog does not want and taking away something it does want. At ACA, rewards, play and your relationship with your dog do most of the work. Any correction is fair, low and clearly taught first, so your dog always knows how to get it right. You can read more about our approach on our About page.

Do I need to be there for my dog's training?

Yes. We train dogs and coach the people who live with them, because a dog only stays trained when its family handles it the same way every day. Each session ends with simple homework, and the work you put in between sessions makes the biggest difference.

What should I bring to my first session?

Bring your dog on the leash and collar you use now, a pouch of treats your dog really likes and some water. It also helps to bring notes on any behaviour you are worried about, such as when it happens and what sets it off. If your dog has any health issues, let us know before the session.

Puppies

What age should I start puppy training?

As soon as your puppy comes home. Our Puppy Foundation course is built for puppies 8 to 16 weeks old, the window when habits form fastest. Older puppies and adult dogs can start with a consultation, or with Basic Obedience once it launches.

What does the Puppy Foundation course cover?

Puppy Foundation covers socialization and confidence, an introduction to leash manners, sit, down, stay and come, and bite inhibition and impulse control. Training is mostly rewards, with a few very mild corrections that we show you first. See the full outline on the Puppy Foundation page.

How do I stop my puppy from biting?

Puppy biting is normal, but it needs to be shaped early. Keep a toy ready so you can give the mouth something better to do, end play calmly when teeth land on skin, and keep sessions short so your puppy does not get overtired and wild. Bite inhibition and impulse control are a core part of Puppy Foundation.

Do you use e-collars on puppies?

Only as an option, and only gradually. At first the puppy simply wears the collar during meals, play and training until it feels normal. Low-level stimulation, always paired with rewards, is added later, once your puppy is old enough and ready.

How should I socialize my puppy?

Aim for calm, good experiences rather than as many as possible. Let your puppy watch new people, dogs, sounds and places from a comfortable distance, reward calm curiosity and leave before things get overwhelming. On our property puppies also learn to stay settled around chickens and ducks, which helps a lot for life in a rural area.

Obedience

What is the difference between Basic Obedience and Advanced Obedience?

Basic Obedience builds core manners for daily life: sit, stay, heel, recall, loose-leash walking and polite greetings. Advanced Obedience raises the standard with distance and duration work, distraction proofing and off-leash reliability goals. Each course page lists what your dog needs to show to graduate.

How do I teach my dog to walk on a loose leash?

Start somewhere quiet, reward your dog for staying near your leg and stop moving forward the moment the leash goes tight. Gentle leash pressure is something a dog learns to switch off by choosing the right spot beside you. Short, frequent sessions work better than one long walk, and loose-leash walking is covered in Basic Obedience.

How do I get my dog to come when called every time?

Practise recall on a long line so your dog can never practise ignoring you, and make coming back the best thing that happens on every walk. We keep dogs on a long line until they are reliable, and only then work toward off leash. Recall is trained in Basic Obedience and proofed in Advanced Obedience.

Can an older dog still learn obedience?

Yes. Older dogs learn well when sessions are short and suited to their body, and many settle into training faster than puppies do. We start with a consultation to see what your dog is comfortable with. Old Dogs New Tricks, by Ryan R. McKenzie, Founder and Head Trainer, is written for exactly these dogs and is listed in our Store.

How long does it take to train a dog?

It depends on the dog, its history and how much practice happens at home. Simple manners can improve within a few sessions, while reliable off-leash work takes much longer. We would rather give you an honest timeline after meeting your dog than promise a number up front.

E-collars

Are e-collars cruel?

Used the way we use them, no. We treat a modern e-collar as a communication tool, run at the lowest level the dog can notice, which feels more like a tap on the shoulder than anything sharp. It is paired with commands the dog already knows, it stops the moment the dog responds, and the response is always rewarded. The collar is never used to zap a dog for ordinary misbehaving.

What level do you use on an e-collar?

We look for each dog's working level: the lowest setting where it shows a small sign of noticing, like an ear flick or a turn of the head. On a 0 to 100 dial, most dogs in a quiet setting work somewhere from the low single digits into the teens. It changes with collar fit and surroundings, so we watch the dog, not the number.

Which e-collar brand do you recommend?

Our first choice is E-Collar Technologies, especially the Educator line, for its fine 0 to 100 control and smooth stimulation. Dogtra is our other recommendation. Cheap collars often jump unevenly between levels, which makes low-level work much harder. See the collars we carry on our E-Collar Technologies page.

Can I buy an E-Collar Technologies collar from you?

Yes. Acadia Canine Academy is an Authorized E-Collar Technologies Dealer, and online ordering is coming soon. In the meantime, contact us and we can help you choose the right system, order it for you and show you how to fit and use it.

Should I start using an e-collar on my own?

We recommend against it. In untrained hands, however good the intentions, an e-collar can hurt a dog physically and mentally. We ask clients to keep their own e-collar work to the low, reward-paired level we teach, and only once we have shown them how to use it properly.

Behaviour modification

Do you work with reactive or aggressive dogs?

Yes. Our Rehabilitation program is for dogs that react to dogs or people, show fear, guard food or objects, or have become hard to manage. It starts with an honest assessment, puts safety and management first and measures progress over time. A consultation is the place to start.

What is behaviour modification?

Behaviour modification means changing how a dog feels about and responds to the things that set it off, not just teaching it to obey. It uses obedience as one tool, along with management, careful exposure and clear communication. The goal is a dog that can stay in control in the places where it used to lose it.

Can you guarantee my dog's behaviour will be fixed?

No honest trainer can. Behaviour can change with health, age and environment, and some dogs will always need some management. What we can offer is a clear plan and progress measured against your own dog's starting point.

My dog guards food or toys. Can you help?

Yes. Resource guarding is common and often improves with a careful plan. Until we meet, avoid taking things away from your dog by force and keep children away from it while it eats or chews. Book a consultation through our contact form and we will assess it with safety first.

When should I see a vet before a trainer?

See your vet first if the behaviour started suddenly, if your dog seems to be in pain, or if it is eating, sleeping or moving differently. Pain and illness can cause snapping, guarding and fear. When a health concern comes up during training, we refer you to your veterinarian before training continues.

Yes. In-home sessions are $80 CAD per hour, with travel included anywhere in the Municipality of Clare. Training at home lets us work on problems where they actually happen, such as the front door, the kitchen or your own street. Book a home visit through the contact form.
