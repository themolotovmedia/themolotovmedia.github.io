E-Collar Myths and the Truth About Low-Level Training

By Ryan R. McKenzie, Founder and Head Trainer, Acadia Canine Academy

Few training tools get people as worked up as the e-collar. Some of that worry is fair. A poor-quality collar in the wrong hands can hurt a dog, and we will never pretend otherwise. But much of what people believe about e-collars comes from old equipment and old methods that have little to do with how we use them today.

Here are the myths we hear most often, and how low-level e-collar training actually works at Acadia Canine Academy.

Myth: An e-collar is a shock collar for punishing dogs

We think of a modern e-collar as a communication tool. At the levels we use, the sensation is more like a tap on the shoulder than anything sharp. It is paired with commands the dog already knows, it stops the moment the dog responds, and the response is always rewarded. For example: touch on, give the cue, let go the instant the dog does it, then "yes" and pay. We mix in reps with and without the touch, and with and without food. Over time the dog learns that the collar simply means "listen, there is something good in this for you."

The collar is never used to zap a dog for ordinary misbehaving, like chewing a shoe or barking at the mail. And while a dog is still learning, we do not use the collar to correct breaks; we block with our body, reset and reward effort.

Myth: E-collars are always used on high settings

Every dog starts by simply wearing the collar during meals, play and training until it feels normal. Only then do we look for the dog's lowest working level: the lowest setting the dog notices and responds to, with a small sign like an ear flick or a turn of the head. We find it again at the start of every session and in every new place, because the same dog needs more near a deer than in the kitchen.

On a 0 to 100 dial, most dogs in a quiet setting work somewhere from the low single digits into the teens. It varies by dog, by collar fit and by how exciting the surroundings are, so we watch the dog, not the number. Ryan R. McKenzie, our Founder and Head Trainer, tests every collar on himself first.

Myth: An e-collar replaces real training

An e-collar does not teach a dog anything on its own. A dog has to understand a command before the collar can help communicate it. That is why the foundation work, with rewards, play and clear handling, always comes first. The collar becomes useful later, especially at a distance, where a leash cannot reach and your voice may get lost.

Myth: Any e-collar will do

Quality matters a great deal. Cheap collars often have uneven output and big jumps between levels, which makes low-level work much harder. Our first choice is E-Collar Technologies, the Educator line in particular, for its fine 0 to 100 control and smooth, gentle stimulation. Dogtra is our other recommendation. Acadia Canine Academy is an Authorized E-Collar Technologies Dealer, so we can help you choose the right system and fit it properly.

Myth: E-collars are the same as old training tricks

Many trainers once recommended the rattle can: a soda can filled with pennies or stones, shaken or even thrown at a dog to startle it away from the counter. Few people thought twice about it. A well-conditioned e-collar at a low level is far quieter and more precise than that, and it gives the dog a clear way to turn the sensation off by responding.

Myth: Puppies should never wear one

In our Puppy Foundations course the e-collar is optional. When families choose it, the puppy just wears the collar at first so it becomes part of everyday life. Very low-level stimulation, paired with rewards, is introduced later, once the puppy is old enough and ready.

The honest part

We are a balanced program, and we would rather be upfront about it. Good training draws on all four ways dogs learn, and rewards and your relationship with your dog do most of the work. For some dogs with serious, long-standing behaviour problems, there is still a place for higher levels, used carefully and briefly as part of a full behaviour modification plan. That work belongs in our Behaviour Modification program, with close supervision.

In untrained hands, however good the intentions, an e-collar can hurt a dog physically and mentally. That is why we ask clients to keep their own e-collar work to the low, reward-paired level we teach, and only once we have shown them how to use it properly.

Why it matters

Used correctly, an e-collar can give a dog more freedom, not less. Reliable off-leash work means more time running in open fields, more trust from the people around them and more places a dog can go with its family. We still keep dogs on a long line until they are reliable, and the e-collar is one of the tools that helps get them there.
