Rehabilitation

Reactive dog training and behaviour rehabilitation in Meteghan, Nova Scotia: structured support for reactive, aggressive, and high-needs dogs, with clarity and realistic expectations.

Separate track Coming Soon

Overview

Notice, process, respond, recover.

Rehabilitation is for dogs whose behaviour has moved beyond a simple obedience problem. The purpose is not just to make the dog obedient. It is to change your dog’s ability to function, so it can notice something difficult, process it, respond appropriately, recover and re-engage with you.

A dog does not need to become indifferent to everything around it. It needs to be able to operate in that environment without losing control. Rehabilitation does not replace obedience; it uses obedience as one of its tools.

Who it is for

  • Dogs that react toward dogs or people, or lunge, bark and explode on leash
  • Dogs that show fear, avoidance or shut down under pressure
  • Dogs that guard food, objects, space or people
  • Dogs that become over-aroused, chase or fixate, or struggle with handling
  • Dogs with severe impulse control problems, or that have become difficult or unsafe to manage

What is covered

Assess first, then train.

What the program includes

  • Behaviour assessment and plan: full history, triggers, early warning signs and risk, then a written plan with measurable targets
  • Threshold management: training at a level where your dog can still think
  • Engagement, disengagement, alternative behaviours and carefully controlled exposure
  • Humane, structured rehabilitation: reward-based methods in exposure work, and no flooding
  • Veterinary input where health may be a factor

What you will learn

  • Handler safety and confidence
  • A management and safety plan for your household
  • How to read your dog’s early warning signs
  • How to create distance and help your dog recover
  • Exercises you can do safely between sessions
  • When to contact us

How it works

Safety and management first.

  1. Behaviour assessment and plan. We collect the full history, identify triggers and early warning signs, assess risk and decide whether the case can be safely taken on. We assess before we prescribe.
  2. Handler safety and management. Before training begins, we put a management plan in place so the problem is not rehearsed, and we build your safety skills and confidence. Management is not failure. It is what makes progress possible.
  3. Veterinary input where needed. Pain and other medical issues can drive behaviour. A sudden or significant change in behaviour calls for a veterinary check, and some cases need a specialist.
  4. Threshold work and measured progress. We work below threshold and track trigger responses, working distances and, above all, how quickly your dog recovers.
Format and pricing: An assessment first, then a written plan with regular reviews. Every case is different, so there is no fixed length. Full program details and pricing will follow. Consultations are available now at $60 CAD/hour, and sessions can also be held at your home in Meteghan or on the French Shore for $75 CAD/hour, travel included. Book a home visit.

Expectations for graduation

Measured progress, not a cure.

Rehabilitation does not end with a “cured” dog. Behaviour can change with health, age and environment, and some management may always be needed. What rehabilitation can deliver is measurable progress, judged against your own dog’s starting point. Progress is tracked through four milestones, reached in order. Each one is met when all of its criteria hold at two reviews in a row.

  • 1. StabilizedYour household is safe and the problem is no longer rehearsed every day: management followed consistently, no bites or serious safety events for 4 weeks, incidents cut by at least half, and you can run the recovery routine.
  • 2. FunctionalYour dog can work around its main trigger in controlled setups: the comfortable working distance is at least 25% shorter, recovery time is cut at least in half, foundation skills work near the trigger, and no safety events for 8 weeks.
  • 3. GeneralizedThe progress holds in real life: in at least 3 environments, with different versions of the trigger, and through unplanned encounters, with no safety events for 12 weeks and every handler following the plan.
  • 4. Maintenance-readyProgram completion: progress holds for a further 4 weeks, you can plan and run sessions on your own, you know your dog’s triggers, warning signs and management rules, and a written maintenance plan is in place.

The numbers above are ACA defaults. The trainer may adjust them for an individual case, and any change is recorded.

Setbacks happen. A safety event or a slide back toward old behaviour means tightening management and returning to the last milestone your dog still meets. A setback is information, not failure.

After the program: some dogs move on to Basic Obedience once they are stable enough, usually from Milestone 2 or 3. Others continue with their maintenance plan and scheduled check-ins.

ACA does not promise to fix every dog, and no specific outcome can be guaranteed. The goal is honest, measurable improvement: more functional behaviour, better handler control, reduced risk and a long-term plan that works for your family.
